Making Tax Digital (MTD) for Income Tax is changing the way self-employed people report their income and expenses to HMRC. If you’re a self-employed builder, plumber, electrician, carpenter or other tradesperson, you may already need to follow the new rules, or you could be required to join from April 2027 or April 2028.

The good news is that preparing for Making Tax Digital doesn’t have to be complicated or expensive. With the right bookkeeping software and a straightforward system for keeping track of your business finances, you can stay organised and meet HMRC’s requirements.

In this guide, we’ll explain who needs to use Making Tax Digital, what records you must keep, how quarterly updates work, and how to get started using FreeAgent bookkeeping software. We’ll also explain what to do if you’ve fallen behind and how MTD Trade Books can help you get back on track.

What Is Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is an HMRC system that requires eligible self-employed people and landlords to keep digital records of their business income and expenses and send regular updates using compatible software.

Instead of relying entirely on one annual Self Assessment tax return, people within MTD must maintain digital records throughout the year and submit quarterly updates to HMRC.

These quarterly updates summarise your income and expenses. They are not four separate tax returns, and you do not have to pay Income Tax four times a year. Your normal tax payment deadlines generally remain the same.

At the end of the tax year, you must also complete the required year-end reporting to finalise your taxable income and tax position.

For builders and tradespeople, this means keeping track of payments received from customers, materials purchased, tools, fuel and other allowable business expenses using suitable digital bookkeeping software.

Who Needs to Use Making Tax Digital in 2026, 2027 and 2028?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is being introduced gradually, depending on how much qualifying income you receive from self-employment and property.

HMRC has set the following thresholds:

• From 6 April 2026: You must use MTD if your qualifying income was over **£50,000** in the 2024/25 tax year.

• From 6 April 2027: The threshold reduces to over **£30,000**, based on qualifying income in the 2025/26 tax year.

• From 6 April 2028: The threshold reduces again to over**£20,000**, based on qualifying income in the 2026/27 tax year.

Importantly, these thresholds are based on your gross qualifying income before expenses, not your profit.

For example, if you’re a self-employed builder with £55,000 in annual turnover and £25,000 in allowable business expenses, your profit might only be £30,000. However, it is your qualifying income of £55,000 that determines whether you fall within the MTD threshold.

If you have both self-employment and property income, HMRC generally combines the relevant gross income from both sources when assessing whether you must join.

Certain exemptions and special circumstances may apply, so it’s important to check your individual position with HMRC.

How Can Self-Employed Builders and Tradespeople Prepare for MTD?

If you’re a self-employed builder, plumber, electrician, carpenter, decorator or other tradesperson, preparing for Making Tax Digital doesn’t need to be difficult. The key is getting your bookkeeping organised before your first quarterly update is due.

Here are five practical steps to help you prepare.

1. Use a Separate Business Bank Account

Having a dedicated business bank account makes it much easier to keep track of money coming into and going out of your business. It also helps separate business transactions from personal spending.

Some business bank accounts, including eligible accounts with NatWest and Metro Bank, provide access to FreeAgent bookkeeping software at no additional subscription cost, subject to the bank’s terms.

2. Keep Records of Your Income

Record payments received from customers, including payments for building work, plumbing, electrical work, decorating and other services.

If you’re a subcontractor working under the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS), you’ll also need to keep records of your CIS deductions.

3. Keep Track of Business Expenses

Keep digital records of materials, tools, fuel, vehicle expenses, insurance, equipment and other allowable business costs.

Retaining receipts and invoices helps support your records and makes it easier to calculate your taxable profit.

4. Start Using Compatible Bookkeeping Software

Making Tax Digital requires digital record-keeping and compatible software to submit information to HMRC.

FreeAgent can connect to supported business bank accounts, helping you organise transactions, categorise income and expenses, and prepare the information needed for your quarterly updates.

5. Keep Your Bookkeeping Up to Date

Rather than leaving everything until the end of the tax year, get into the habit of reviewing your transactions regularly.

Keeping your records up to date throughout the year makes quarterly reporting much easier and reduces the risk of missing important deadlines.

What Happens If You Miss an MTD Quarterly Update?

If you’ve missed a Making Tax Digital quarterly update deadline, the most important thing is to get your bookkeeping up to date and submit any outstanding information as soon as possible.

Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, eligible self-employed people must submit quarterly updates to HMRC using compatible software, even if they have very little business activity during the period.

Will HMRC Fine You for Missing an MTD Quarterly Update?

HMRC operates a points-based penalty system for late submissions. Under the quarterly reporting rules, a missed deadline can result in a penalty point.

Once you reach the relevant penalty threshold, you can receive a £200 financial penalty. Further late submissions may result in additional penalties.

However, HMRC has introduced a temporary relaxation for the first year of mandatory Making Tax Digital for Income Tax. For the 2026/27 tax year, penalty points will not be issued for late quarterly updates.

This does not mean quarterly updates are optional. You must still submit the required information and complete your year-end tax obligations.

Your bookkeeping records can then be brought up to date using compatible software such as FreeAgent, allowing outstanding quarterly updates to be prepared and submitted.
