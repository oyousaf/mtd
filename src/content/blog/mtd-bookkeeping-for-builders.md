## What Is Making Tax Digital for Builders?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is an HMRC system requiring eligible self-employed people to keep digital records of their business income and expenses.

Rather than relying entirely on an annual Self Assessment tax return, those within MTD must use compatible software to maintain their records and send quarterly updates to HMRC.

These updates summarise your income and expenses throughout the tax year. You must still complete an end-of-year tax return.

For a builder, that could mean keeping digital records of:

- Payments received from customers and contractors
- Building materials and supplies
- Tools and equipment
- Van and allowable travel expenses
- Business insurance
- Other allowable business expenses
- CIS deductions made by contractors

Keeping these records up to date makes it easier to understand how your business is performing and prepare for tax deadlines.

## Which Builders Need to Use Making Tax Digital in 2026 and 2027?

For more information, read our complete guide on [how to get ready for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax if you’re self-employed.](/blog/how-to-get-ready-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax-if-you-re-self-employed)

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is being introduced gradually, depending on how much qualifying income you earn from self-employment and property.

The current timetable is:

- **From 6 April 2026:** Qualifying income over £50,000.
- **From 6 April 2027:** Qualifying income over £30,000.
- **From 6 April 2028:** Qualifying income over £20,000.

Qualifying income generally means your total gross income from self-employment and property before deducting business expenses, rather than your taxable profit.

HMRC uses information from previous tax returns to determine when you must start using Making Tax Digital.

For example, if you’re a self-employed builder with £38,000 in qualifying income for the 2025/26 tax year, you would normally need to start using MTD from 6 April 2027, unless an exemption or other exception applies.

This means more builders, plumbers, electricians, carpenters and CIS subcontractors will need to prepare for digital bookkeeping before April 2027.

If you’re a self-employed builder or subcontractor working under the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS), you may already be familiar with contractors deducting tax from your payments before you receive them.

Under Making Tax Digital, eligible CIS subcontractors must also maintain digital records and submit quarterly income and expense updates to HMRC.

## What Happens If You’ve Missed an MTD Quarterly Deadline?

If you’re a self-employed builder, plumber, electrician or CIS subcontractor and you’ve missed a Making Tax Digital quarterly deadline, the important thing is not to ignore it.

Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, eligible sole traders must submit quarterly updates of their business income and expenses to HMRC using compatible software.

HMRC operates a points-based penalty system for late submissions. Generally, a late quarterly update can result in one penalty point, with a £200 financial penalty once the four-point threshold is reached.

However, **HMRC has introduced a transitional arrangement for the first year of mandatory MTD for Income Tax**, meaning penalty points will not be issued for late quarterly updates during the 2026/27 tax year.

This doesn’t mean you can ignore the deadlines. You’re still required to keep digital records and submit your quarterly updates.

## When Are MTD Quarterly Updates Due?

For a more detailed explanation of quarterly reporting, read our guide to [MTD Quarterly Updates for Builders.](/blog/mtd-quarterly-updates-for-builders-what-do-you-need-to-submit)

Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, self-employed builders and CIS subcontractors who fall within the new rules must send quarterly updates of their business income and expenses to HMRC.

These updates help keep your tax records organised throughout the year rather than leaving everything until the annual tax return deadline.

### Making Tax Digital Quarterly Deadlines

For businesses using standard tax-year reporting periods, the deadlines are:

- **Quarter 1:** 6 April to 5 July – submission deadline 7 August.
- **Quarter 2:** 6 July to 5 October – submission deadline 7 November.
- **Quarter 3:** 6 October to 5 January – submission deadline 7 February.
- **Quarter 4:** 6 January to 5 April – submission deadline 7 May.

Some businesses may choose calendar-quarter reporting periods instead, although the submission deadlines remain the same.

### Do You Still Need to Complete an Annual Tax Return?

Yes. Making Tax Digital does not remove the requirement to complete an annual tax return.

Your quarterly updates provide HMRC with information about your income and expenses throughout the year, but you must still finalise your tax position through your annual tax return.

The usual deadline for submitting an online tax return remains **31 January following the end of the tax year**.
