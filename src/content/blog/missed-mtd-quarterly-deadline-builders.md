## What Are the Making Tax Digital Quarterly Deadlines for Builders?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax requires eligible self-employed people to keep digital records of their business income and expenses and submit regular quarterly updates using compatible software.

For those using standard tax-year reporting periods, the deadlines are:

- **7 August** — first quarterly update.
- **7 November** — second quarterly update.
- **7 February** — third quarterly update.
- **7 May** — fourth quarterly update.

These dates apply to the relevant tax year, with the February and May deadlines falling in the following calendar year.

Quarterly updates summarise your recorded income and expenses. They are not separate tax returns, and you don’t normally pay income tax simply because a quarterly update is due.

## What Happens If You Miss an MTD Quarterly Deadline in 2026?

This is particularly important for builders who joined Making Tax Digital on 6 April 2026.

**HMRC will not issue late-submission penalty points for quarterly updates relating to the 2026/27 tax year.**

That means if you missed the first deadline on 7 August 2026, or miss another quarterly deadline for that tax year, you won’t receive a quarterly late-submission penalty point.

But this doesn’t mean you can ignore your bookkeeping.

You must still:

- Keep the required digital records.
- Complete any outstanding quarterly updates.
- Use compatible software to submit the information.
- Complete your annual tax return by the relevant deadline.
- Pay any tax due on time.

For example, if you’re a builder who should have submitted your first quarterly update by 7 August 2026 but haven’t done so, you should still get your records organised and submit the outstanding update.

The temporary penalty easement is intended to help people adjust to Making Tax Digital, not remove the reporting requirements.

**Important:** The 2026/27 easement applies to late quarterly updates. Penalties can still apply to late annual tax returns and late tax payments.

You can read the full rules in [HMRC’s official Making Tax Digital penalties guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/penalties-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax).

## Will Builders Get Fined for Missing MTD Deadlines in 2027?

From the 2027/28 tax year, the normal points-based late-submission system applies to quarterly updates, under the rules currently announced.

This is particularly relevant to builders and tradespeople who are already within Making Tax Digital.

HMRC’s system works differently from the old approach of immediately issuing a fixed penalty for every missed deadline.

Instead, you normally receive a penalty point when you miss a relevant submission deadline.

Once you reach the penalty threshold, a financial penalty becomes payable.

### How the MTD Penalty Points System Works

For taxpayers required to use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, the normal late-submission threshold is four points.

For example:

- **First missed deadline:** One penalty point.
- **Second missed deadline:** Two points in total.
- **Third missed deadline:** Three points in total.
- **Fourth missed deadline:** Four points, triggering a £200 penalty.
- **Further missed deadlines while at the threshold:** Normally another £200 penalty for each missed submission deadline.

The points system also covers relevant late annual tax returns.

Points can expire or be removed after the required period of compliance, subject to HMRC’s rules.

This means an occasional mistake doesn’t necessarily lead to an immediate financial penalty, but repeatedly missing deadlines can become expensive.

The special no-penalty-points arrangement is confirmed for quarterly updates in 2026/27. It should not be assumed that everyone joining MTD in later years automatically receives the same concession.

The first step is to make sure your digital bookkeeping records are complete and accurate.

- CIS deductions and payment statements.

Once the records are organised, you can use compatible software to prepare and submit the outstanding update.

Don’t simply guess the figures to get something submitted quickly.

Accurate records are important for your annual tax position and for avoiding further problems later.

## What Happens If You’re a CIS Subcontractor and Miss an MTD Deadline?

CIS subcontractors have the same general Making Tax Digital quarterly reporting obligations as other eligible self-employed people.

However, their bookkeeping can be more complicated because contractors often deduct CIS tax before making payment.

For example, suppose your contractor pays you:

- Gross labour earnings: **£2,000**
- CIS tax deducted: **£400**
- Net payment into your bank: **£1,600**

Your bookkeeping needs to reflect the gross earnings and CIS deduction correctly.

Simply recording the £1,600 bank receipt as your total gross earnings could understate your income.

If you’ve missed an MTD deadline, it’s important to organise both your business transactions and CIS deduction records before completing your outstanding updates.

## Do You Still Need to Complete Your Annual Tax Return If You’ve Missed Quarterly Updates?

Yes. Missing quarterly updates doesn’t remove your obligation to complete your annual tax return.

Under Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, eligible taxpayers must keep digital records, send the required quarterly updates and complete the annual tax return process using compatible software.

The usual annual tax return deadline remains **31 January following the end of the tax year**.

For example, the tax return for 2026/27 is due by **31 January 2028**.

You will need to complete your outstanding quarterly updates before submitting your annual tax return through the MTD process.

Late annual tax returns can result in penalty points and financial penalties, even where the special 2026/27 quarterly-update easement applies.

Late tax payments are subject to separate rules, including interest and potential penalties.

This is why it’s sensible to deal with missed quarterly updates well before the annual tax return deadline.

## Who Needs to Use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is being introduced gradually according to qualifying income.

The main thresholds are:

- **6 April 2026:** Qualifying income over £50,000.
- **6 April 2027:** Qualifying income over £30,000.
- **6 April 2028:** Qualifying income over £20,000.

Qualifying income generally means your combined gross income from self-employment and property, before deducting business expenses.

It is not the same as taxable profit.

The relevant earlier tax year’s income is used to determine when you must join, and exemptions or other exceptions may apply.
